How to Use This Booklet

This booklet accompanies the week of November 8–14, 2026. In the morning, read the biblical passage in your own Bible and carry one concrete response into the day. In the evening, return to the Word to give thanks, recognize what needs attention, and rest in God's grace.

On Sunday, we meditate on the readings from worship. From Monday through Wednesday, we return to Sunday, November 8. From Thursday through Saturday, the daily readings from the semicontinuous track prepare us for worship on November 15.

Each devotional has a cover and three reading pages. In the Story version, the first page presents the biblical reading; the second gathers the reflection, commitment, and prayer. The community A Palavra Entre Nós can also be found on WhatsApp.

November 8 — Morning

Waiting for the One Who Loved Us First

Matthew 25:1–13
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Receiving the Word

Ten young women go out to meet the bridegroom. Jesus tells the parable to disciples who need to learn how to wait. His return is certain, but the waiting may be long. At the center of Christian hope stands the Lord who gave himself for us, rose again, and will come to meet us.

The bridegroom is delayed, and all of them fall asleep. The difference becomes clear when the announcement comes and the waiting has already stretched on. Receiving the Word, praying, and living in fellowship are ways of cultivating, by grace, a faith that can endure the delay.

This Sunday, the community's gathering can be a place of rest and genuine listening. We do not have to bring an appearance of strength that conceals every difficulty. We can confess our need and receive help. Christ's grace comes before our response and makes that response possible.

Living the Hope

Ask what has been postponed in your relationship with God and with your neighbor. Do not draw up an impossible list of changes. Choose one response that has already become clear: seeking a conversation, acknowledging a mistake, or reorganizing a commitment that has become burdensome. Where a relationship has been harmed, making amends requires care, not merely a hurried sentence.

Keeping watch does not mean possessing the future. It means living today before the One in whom we trust. The community can help, but it does not need to treat your conscience as its property. Listen to Christ also when his Word corrects a long-held preference.

Commitment and Prayer

Before you pray, read the parable's final sentence again. Tell the Lord where your courage has weakened during the waiting. Ask him to show you a way back to the Word with the community's help.

Commitment: Set aside a time to listen and choose one possible response to this Sunday's Gospel. If you need help, seek out a trustworthy person without feeling obliged to disclose the situation to everyone.

Prayer: Lord Jesus, I receive your Word with all my limitations. Free me from appearances and indifference. Teach me to await your coming with faith, rest, and faithfulness. May your grace reach my decisions and the care I give to others. Amen.

November 8 — Evening

Hope Can Accompany Our Tears

1 Thessalonians 4:13–18
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Deepening the Reflection

Paul writes to a community that misses those who have died. Because Jesus died and rose again, those who have died in Christ will have their place in the meeting with the Lord. This promise gives hope to those who mourn. At the end of the passage, Paul asks the brothers and sisters to comfort one another with these words.

Perhaps this Sunday brought back a powerful memory. An empty chair, a song, or a gesture can make an absence feel close again. This experience need not be explained as a spiritual failure. Love that has been shared leaves its marks, and the hope of resurrection does not disregard anyone's story.

The comfort Paul encourages may take the form of a visit or patient listening. The promise of resurrection gives substance to our presence. Someone who is suffering may need another person to seek out their company again after the activity of the first few days has passed.

Examining the Day

Was there room for the truth of your feelings? Did you try to hide your longing so as not to disappoint someone? Or did you listen to another person's pain as though it were a problem that had to be closed quickly? Bring these questions before the Lord without condemning yourself for being human.

Give thanks for a cherished memory and for the promise of the risen Christ. Confess any words that hurt someone in the name of comfort, if that happened. Making amends may be simple: acknowledge that you spoke too quickly and offer to listen. You need not turn the conversation into a defense of your intentions.

Resting in Grace

Be silent and remember before Christ someone whose absence you feel. You may give thanks for something you shared and speak of your longing in your own words. If words fail you today, remain in silence before the Lord.

Commitment: Allow hope to accompany grief without demanding that grief disappear. If you would welcome company, arrange a conversation rather than face everything alone.

Prayer: Risen Christ, before you I place the names of those I miss. Receive my tears and sustain those who are grieving. Give us truthful words and patient presence. As we await the fullness of life, keep us in your grace. Amen.

November 9 — Morning

A Memory That Becomes Faithfulness

Joshua 24:25–33
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Receiving the Word

At Shechem, Joshua records the words of the covenant and sets up a stone as a witness. Farewells and burials follow. One generation passes, and another must learn why those words deserve to be kept. The stone recalls the commitment made before the God who had already cared for the people.

Yesterday's parable called us to faithful waiting. At Shechem, the memory of God's works helps the people take their response seriously. At home or in church, a story told honestly can help someone understand why keeping one's word matters.

God's faithfulness outlasts any leader's tenure. That allows us to give thanks for those who served without treating their presence as an absolute guarantee of the community's life. The Lord remains the center of our trust when beloved people are no longer with us.

Living the Hope

Return to a commitment you made yesterday. Is it clear what you intend to do and what help you need? A vague promise can remain forever in the future. Give it a workable form, respecting limitations and listening to those who will be affected. If making the promise was unwise, acknowledge that and adjust it honestly.

Christian remembrance does not need to conceal failures in order to preserve gratitude. We can remember the good we have received and correct what was unjust. History belongs to God; we do not need to defend the perfection of every choice we have made.

Commitment and Prayer

Give thanks for someone who taught you to keep your word. Remember a specific gesture of theirs. As you pray, place today's responsibility before the Lord and ask for humility to speak truthfully about what you can undertake.

Commitment: Write down one responsibility for this week and agree on a simple way of following through. Do not use the record to control another person's life.

Prayer: Faithful God, thank you for those who helped us come to know your Word. Keep us from a nostalgia that prevents us from hearing the present. Give us integrity to fulfill what we promise and humility to correct what needs to change. Sustain our journey in Christ. Amen.

November 9 — Evening

Words the Other Person Can Understand

1 Corinthians 14:20–25
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Deepening the Reflection

Paul imagines someone entering a church gathering. If what that person hears is incomprehensible, how will they recognize what God is doing there? A word that builds up the church can also reach someone who comes from outside. In the scene the apostle describes, the visitor listens, finds their heart laid bare, and recognizes God's presence.

Psalm 78, heard on Sunday, calls us to tell new generations what God has done. An expression familiar to longtime members may need explanation. Listening to the questions of someone who has just arrived is part of the care involved in passing on the faith.

Speaking clearly does not mean impoverishing the Gospel. A profound truth can be explained in concrete words and patiently revisited. Love considers the hearer and is willing to learn when its own communication has failed.

Examining the Day

In any conversation, did you assume the other person already understood? Was there haste, overly technical language, or a lack of listening? Perhaps someone explained something to you patiently. Give thanks for that person and consider how to offer the same care.

If something you said caused confusion, clarify it without belittling the person who asked. We can also confess when we have used knowledge as a way of asserting superiority. Grace frees us to serve with what we know and to acknowledge what we still need to study.

Resting in Grace

If a conversation today still troubles you, remember what the other person actually said. Before preparing your answer, ask the Spirit to give you the attention needed to understand a question you may have missed.

Commitment: Tomorrow, ask someone whether an important instruction was clear, making room for an honest answer.

Prayer: Spirit of God, purify the way we speak. May the Word be proclaimed truthfully and received with understanding. Forgive the vanity that can accompany our knowledge. Give us attentive ears and patience. At the end of this day, we entrust to you the conversations that still need care. Amen.

November 10 — Morning

Understanding the Word and Sharing Joy

Nehemiah 8:1–12
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Receiving the Word

In Jerusalem, the people gather to hear the Law. The Levites explain what is read, and the assembly begins to understand. When the Word reaches the listeners, they weep. On that holy day, they are instructed to celebrate and send portions to those who have nothing prepared.

The command to share food shows how far the joy of the Lord extends. The person who arrives with nothing prepared also has a place at the table. A community that has understood the Word begins to notice who might otherwise be left out of the celebration.

Psalm 78, heard on Sunday, called us to pass on the memory of God. Nehemiah shows a people learning together. We can accept instruction without being ashamed of beginning. No one needs to hide the fact that they have not understood a passage in order to maintain a religious image.

Living the Hope

Choose a biblical passage that has raised a question for you and take time to study it calmly. Consider its context before looking for a ready-made answer. A teacher can help you without taking the place of your own listening.

Ask also who is not sharing in the community's joy. Someone may lack resources, transportation, or companionship. Sharing requires discovering what is needed, not merely imagining what would be good to offer. Ask and agree on a form of help that is possible.

Commitment and Prayer

As you pray, think of someone who tends to remain on the margins of your community's celebrations. Choose one welcoming gesture that fits your day. If you do not know what to offer, begin by asking what the person needs.

Commitment: Write down one question about the reading and carry out an act of sharing that respects the person receiving it.

Prayer: Lord, open our understanding to your Word. May it correct sin and renew our joy. Give us sensitivity toward those left on the margins and a willingness to share with integrity. Teach us to celebrate your grace as a community that learns together. Amen.

November 10 — Evening

Love That Grows While We Wait

1 Thessalonians 3:6–13
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Deepening the Reflection

Timothy brings news of the Thessalonians' faith and love, and Paul is encouraged in the midst of his afflictions. The apostle gives thanks and prays that the church's love may increase, both among brothers and sisters and toward everyone. This is how he hopes to find the community prepared for the Lord's coming.

We can wait for news and, in the meantime, forget to nurture a relationship. The letter shows the importance of asking, giving thanks, and wishing others well. We do not need to control someone's path in order to continue loving them. Care can remain respectful even when we do not have all the information.

On Sunday, the hope of resurrection sustained mutual consolation. Today, Paul connects Christ's coming with a love that grows during the waiting. The Lord strengthens hearts for a holiness that becomes visible in how we treat others.

Examining the Day

From whom did you receive good news? For whom were you able to give thanks? Was a relationship treated only as a matter of demands, without room to recognize the grace already at work? Name this before God.

Perhaps a message has gone unanswered. Do not immediately interpret silence as rejection. There are circumstances you do not know. You may need to reach out again gently or respect a boundary that has been communicated. Love does not require uninterrupted access to someone else's life.

Resting in Grace

Bring before the Lord someone from whom you are distant. Give thanks for a concrete memory of that relationship and ask for that person's good. Lay before God your anxiety about an answer you have not yet received.

Commitment: Give thanks for someone and, if appropriate, send them a simple word of encouragement without demanding a reply.

Prayer: God of love, strengthen the bonds we have received and heal the way we relate to one another. Give us gratitude for what is good and patience with what we do not understand. Cause our love to grow without control or indifference. Kept in Christ, we entrust this night to you. Amen.

November 11 — Morning

The Word That Remains When Everything Seems to Change

Matthew 24:29–35
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Receiving the Word

Jesus speaks of the coming of the Son of Man in images that shake heaven and earth. He then reminds the disciples of the fig tree, whose branches announce the approaching season. Above all that changes, Christ's word remains. In that word, the disciples can anchor their hope.

After hearing the parable of the young women, we might ask whether the delay means abandonment. In the same discourse, Jesus says that the day and hour remain unknown (Matthew 24:36). The announcement of the future sustains faithfulness in the present.

A change at work, in the family, or in the community can unsettle what we rely on. Not every crisis corresponds to the images in this passage. We do not need to turn the news into a secret code in order to maintain our faith. We can seek the facts, act prudently, and trust Christ.

Living the Hope

Notice what has been capturing your attention. An alarming message may seem more convincing than patient reading. Before forwarding a religious prediction, check its source and ask whether it corresponds to what Jesus actually said.

The Word that remains also corrects us. It does not exist merely to confirm what we want. Receive one clear instruction from Scripture and consider one possible response today. Faithfulness has more substance than a continual search for frightening novelties.

Commitment and Prayer

Turn away from the news for a moment and reread Matthew 24:35. Name before Christ the concern that most demands your attention today. After praying, choose the care that lies within your reach and leave the future in his hands.

Commitment: Stop sharing an unverified report and set aside time to read Matthew 24 in context.

Prayer: Lord Jesus, your Word remains. Guard us from the fear that seeks certainty where you have not given it. Teach us to discern, listen, and serve in the present. When familiar things change, sustain our trust in your faithfulness. Amen.

November 11 — Evening

A Covenant That Reaches the Heart

Jeremiah 31:31–34
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Deepening the Reflection

Jeremiah announces a new covenant to the house of Israel and the house of Judah. God promises to write his law on their hearts, make himself known, and forgive sin. The Lord takes the initiative in restoring communion. The promise reaches the place where our desires and choices take shape.

Joshua's commitment, heard on Sunday, raised the question of whom we serve. Jeremiah prevents us from concluding that a well-formulated response can, by itself, resolve the condition of the heart. We depend on the grace that forgives and transforms. In Christ, the community receives the hope of the new covenant.

The promise was announced to Israel and Judah within their own history. At the Lord's Supper, Christians receive it in the light of Jesus' words about the new covenant (Luke 22:20). Remembering both passages helps us confess Christ's grace with gratitude for Israel's history.

Examining the Day

Was there a gap between what you declared and what you desired? We can fulfill an obligation outwardly while nurturing resentment within. Examining the day does not require resolving this contradiction by ourselves. It leads us to confess it to the God who forgives.

Give thanks for a change that grace has already made possible, however small. Do not despise a beginning because a struggle remains. Nor should you use the struggle as an excuse to stop listening. The Lord works in real people, with histories and limitations.

Resting in Grace

Recall before God one thing you said wholeheartedly and another you said only formally. Confess what needs to change. Read Jeremiah 31:34 again and receive the promise of forgiveness before ending the day.

Commitment: Bring before God an area of your heart in need of renewal, and seek support when change requires ongoing accompaniment.

Prayer: God of the covenant, forgive our divided hearts and write your Word into our lives. In Jesus Christ we receive your mercy. May your Spirit renew our desires and teach us grateful obedience. We rest in the grace that we cannot produce ourselves. Amen.

November 12 — Morning

Lifting Our Eyes to Receive Mercy

Psalm 123
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Receiving the Word

In Psalm 123, one voice lifts its eyes to God and is soon joined by others in a plea for mercy. The people are weary of contempt. Their prayer brings that humiliation into the presence of the Lord, from whom they expect help. There are days when we can pray these very words.

The reading prepares us for the parable of the talents that we will hear on Sunday. Before asking what we will do with the goods entrusted to us, we can recognize from whom we received them. Asking for mercy teaches us to serve with an awareness of how deeply we depend on God.

The plea for mercy can be repeated when words fail. That is not a sign of an inferior prayer. Faith does not always find lengthy sentences; sometimes it can only lift its eyes. The Lord does not measure our prayer's truth by how many arguments we offer.

Living the Hope

If you have been treated with contempt, you do not need to accept humiliation as the definition of who you are. You may seek support and, when necessary, establish boundaries. Prayer does not require you to remain available to every form of violence.

Ask also whether one of your actions has diminished another person. Contempt may appear in a tone of voice, a joke, or a refusal to listen. Asking for mercy includes allowing God to correct how we exercise strength. The grace we receive must reach our relationships.

Commitment and Prayer

Pray the psalm's first verse slowly. If you can, add the name of someone else who is also awaiting mercy. Carry that person in prayer and think of a way to offer respectful companionship.

Commitment: Pause during the day to ask for mercy and reconsider one concrete way of showing respect for your neighbor.

Prayer: Lord, I lift my eyes to you. Receive my weakness and protect me from contempt. Correct what in me humiliates others and strengthen what is learning to care. Prepare us to serve with the gifts we have received, always dependent on your grace. Amen.

November 12 — Evening

When the Memory of Grace Grows Dim

Judges 2:6–15
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Deepening the Reflection

After the death of Joshua and his generation, Judges speaks of a people who no longer know the Lord or the works he had done for Israel. The rupture appears in worship as well, when Israel turns to other gods. The account invites us to consider how the memory of grace reaches those who are just beginning.

The story exposes Israel's unfaithfulness in the context of its covenant with God. As we hear it today, we should examine how we nurture the memory of divine care. Anyone going through suffering deserves attentive listening and presence. A hurried explanation of the cause of someone's pain can inflict still more hurt.

As we prepare for next Sunday, the reading asks what we do with what has been entrusted to us. Knowledge received can be guarded as private property or shared with care. A community serves the future when it teaches without dominating and listens to those who are beginning.

Examining the Day

Did you help someone understand something, or did you assume they should already know it? Was there an opportunity to speak of God's faithfulness in simple language? Not every conversation needs to become a lesson. A testimony may begin with gratitude and with the way we treat another person.

Give thanks for those who passed the faith on to you. Acknowledge, too, that some lessons were incomplete and some customs need correction. Memory can be honest without losing gratitude. God does not depend on an idealized version of our history in order to remain faithful.

Resting in Grace

Choose a memory of someone speaking patiently to you about God. Give thanks for that person and imagine how to tell the same story in a way that would make sense to someone hearing it for the first time. Leave room for their questions.

Commitment: Choose one memory of grace to share with a younger person, making room for their questions.

Prayer: Faithful God, do not let us forget your care. Forgive our indifference and the haste with which we sometimes teach. Give us understandable words and a life consistent with the Gospel. Keep the new generations and receive our rest in Christ. Amen.

November 13 — Morning

Learning from a History That Repeats Itself

Judges 2:16–23
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Receiving the Word

God hears Israel's groaning and raises up judges to rescue the people. Yet the death of each judge is followed by renewed unfaithfulness. The text allows us to feel the seriousness of this return and the Lord's patience, for he had already answered the cries of people unable to free themselves.

Some of our choices end up repeating harm we have already recognized. When that happens, we would do well to listen to those who have suffered the consequences and seek help to change. Israel's experience reminds us both of God's patience and of the seriousness of repeatedly returning to the same path.

Preparing for the parable of the talents includes asking how we respond to the goods entrusted to us. God's grace is not permission to leave everything as it is. It allows us to face the truth without relying on the illusion that we have never been wrong.

Living the Hope

Identify one recurring situation that is within your reach to examine. Try to distinguish facts, feelings, and decisions. Perhaps you need to listen to someone affected, reorganize a routine, or seek accompaniment. Do not promise a total change in an instant.

If the situation involves other people, respect their boundaries. Repentance does not entitle us to demand immediate trust from someone who has been harmed. Faithfulness can be demonstrated over time through consistent actions. Hope in Christ sustains this patient journey.

Commitment and Prayer

As you pray, tell the Lord what change needs to begin with an action of your own. Ask for the courage to seek help and the patience to carry out the step you choose. Remember that other people may need time before they can trust again.

Commitment: Take one verifiable first step toward a necessary change and arrange a form of accountability or support, when appropriate.

Prayer: Lord, thank you for your mercy that comes to our aid. Give us clarity to recognize paths that need to change. Deliver us from empty promises and sustain a persevering response. May the gifts we have received become service and care in Jesus Christ. Amen.

November 13 — Evening

God's Justice Does Not Authorize Our Revenge

Psalm 123 and Revelation 16:8–21
Read the complete passages in your Bible.

Deepening the Reflection

The images in Revelation 16 are severe. In the midst of judgment, the account shows people who persist in defying God. Psalm 123 puts another response to suffering on our lips: the plea for mercy. Together, the readings help us entrust to the Lord the judgment that belongs to him.

The psalm's prayer gives a voice to those who have endured contempt. Waiting for divine justice allows us to act firmly without taking the Judge's place. We can tell the truth about harm, seek protection for those who have suffered, and ask God for mercy.

As we prepare for next Sunday, we recognize that giving account to God includes how we use our strength and our words. The responsibility entrusted to us does not make us owners of anyone's life. Lordship belongs to Christ.

Examining the Day

Did any indignation turn into a desire to humiliate someone? Did you share an unfounded religious interpretation of a disaster? You may need to correct a report and acknowledge that you spoke beyond what you knew.

Give thanks for an experience of justice or protection. Intercede for those who suffer and for those working to repair harm. Christian hope does not require us to abandon the pursuit of justice in the present. It teaches us to pursue justice without falsehood and to entrust to God what we cannot fully judge.

Resting in Grace

Before going to sleep, bring before the Lord a situation that stirred your indignation. First, intercede for those who were hurt. Ask for wisdom to discern whether tomorrow you should correct a report, seek help, or sustain someone in prayer.

Commitment: Stop words of revenge and replace them with a responsible act of protection, truth-telling, or intercession.

Prayer: Just and merciful God, guard our words. Correct our desire to dominate and uphold those who suffer violence. Give wisdom to those who seek to repair harm. Tonight, we entrust our indignation to you and ask for hearts willing to serve peace in truth. Amen.

November 14 — Morning

A Life Must Be Filled with Grace

Matthew 12:43–45
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Receiving the Word

In the picture Jesus describes, a spirit returns and finds the house empty, swept, and put in order. The final warning is addressed to the generation listening to Jesus. The orderly house helps us perceive how serious it is to remain indifferent to the Kingdom when the One who announces it stands before us.

Matthew places this warning between a discussion about signs and Jesus' encounter with those who hear and do the Father's will. The image must be read within this call to respond. When someone suffers, the Christian community is called to listen and care.

On the eve of the parable of the talents, the question is what fills our lives. Avoiding a mistake matters, but the Christian journey also receives direction, fellowship, and purpose. In Christ, we are called to a renewed life, nourished by the Word and open to service.

Living the Hope

Is there a space you have tried merely to keep empty? After leaving a habit behind, you may need to build a different routine and seek companionship. Change must take relationships, time, and support into account, rather than relying on willpower alone.

Do not turn religious activity into a way of filling every hour. A life filled with grace includes rest, work, prayer, and care. The Lord does not require a packed schedule in order to be acknowledged. He is to be heard in how we receive his gifts and respond to them.

Commitment and Prayer

Set aside a few minutes to listen to the Word before returning to your tasks. If you have been trying to change a habit alone, bring to prayer the name of someone you could ask to walk alongside you. Give thanks to Christ for the care you have received through others.

Commitment: Choose a practice of listening or fellowship that can help sustain a change already begun.

Prayer: Jesus Christ, renew our lives by your grace. Do not let us be satisfied with appearances. Give us direction, companionship, and a willingness to serve. May your Word reach our desires and decisions, preparing us to hear the Gospel tomorrow. Amen.

November 14 — Evening

Gratitude for Those Willing to Serve

Judges 5:1–12
Read the complete passage in your Bible.

Deepening the Reflection

Deborah and Barak sing of what the Lord has done for Israel. Their song also gives thanks for those who offered themselves willingly. In a history marked by conflict, there is room to remember the help received and to name the courage of those who took part.

Tonight, we can receive a question about gratitude: who was willing to serve this week? Perhaps someone prepared a lesson, shared a task, or listened to someone's pain without being noticed. Acknowledging this service does not require turning people into heroes without limits. Gratitude can be accompanied by rest and by sharing responsibilities.

Tomorrow, the semicontinuous track will take us back to Deborah's story (Judges 4:1–7). The Gospel, meanwhile, will tell the parable of the talents. Both readings invite us to remember God's help and consider carefully what we have received for service.

Examining the Day

What were you able to accomplish? What remains unfinished and needs to be taken up honestly? Do not confuse an incomplete task with the whole of your identity. But do not conceal a promise that someone else is still waiting for. Grace allows us to give thanks and make corrections.

If you are carrying more than you can bear, consider how to ask for help. If you are accustomed to leaving everything to the same few people, think of one concrete way to take part. A community matures when it learns to recognize both gifts and limits instead of depending on the exhaustion of a few.

Resting in Grace

Remember a quiet act of service that made this week easier. Give thanks to God for the person who offered it. As you prepare for tomorrow's worship, ask what responsibility you might share so that someone else can also have time to rest.

Commitment: Thank someone for an act of service and prepare to hear Matthew 25:14–30 on Sunday, with freedom to learn.

Prayer: Faithful God, thank you for the care we have received this week. Receive what has been done and correct what needs review. Give rest to the weary and willingness to those who can take part. In Christ, we entrust this night to you and await a new day under your grace. Amen.
